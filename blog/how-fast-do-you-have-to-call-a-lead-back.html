How Fast Do You Have to Call a Lead Back?

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · July 23, 2026 · 4 min read

If you want the honest answer, it is five minutes. Industry data suggests a lead you reach within five minutes is many times more likely to turn into a real conversation than the same lead called back at thirty. Roughly 3 out of 4 buyers hire whoever answers first, not whoever is cheapest. Every hour a new lead sits in your inbox is worth real money, and for most contractors that adds up to thousands a month.

The window is smaller than you think

A homeowner with a busted water heater is not waiting by the phone for you. He filled out three forms and called two numbers. The first person to reach him with a straight answer usually gets the job. Industry studies suggest the odds of connecting with that lead drop hard after the first five minutes and fall off a cliff after the first hour. By the next morning you are calling someone who already booked your competitor.

The lead was never bad. The timing was.

Most of your competitors are slow, which is good news

The average business takes hours to respond to a web lead. Plenty take days. That is the leak, and it is also the opening. You do not have to be the best-reviewed shop in town or the cheapest. You have to be first. Speed is the one place where a two-truck operation beats the big franchise, because the big franchise is routing the lead through a queue while you are already on the phone.

What a slow callback actually costs

Say you get 40 web leads a month and close 1 in 4 of the ones you reach. If half of them go cold before you call back, you did not lose 20 leads. You lost the 5 jobs those leads would have become. Put an average ticket on that and the number gets loud fast. That is money you already paid to generate, walking to the shop that answered while yours rang out.

How to close the gap

You do not need a bigger team. You need the phone answered and the web lead called back inside five minutes, every time, including nights and weekends when most of the calls actually come in. Industry figures suggest a large share of home services calls land outside normal business hours. If those hit voicemail, they hit your competitor next. A live answer, a text-back, an after-hours booking, anything that beats the clock, pays for itself in the jobs it saves.

Common questions

Is five minutes really necessary, or is that just a sales pitch?

Five minutes is the target, not a gimmick. The connect rate falls sharply after it. Even getting from hours down to under an hour recovers jobs you are losing today. Faster is simply worth more.

What if I cannot answer every call myself?

Then the job is making sure something does, every time. A live answering setup or an automated text-back that books the appointment beats voicemail by a wide margin. The goal is that no lead waits, not that you personally catch every ring.

Do after-hours calls really matter that much?

Yes. A large share of home services calls come in evenings and weekends. Those callers have an emergency and no patience. Miss them and most will not call back a second time.

The lead did not go cold. Your callback did, and the job went to whoever was faster.
