The Growth You Already Paid For

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · October 22, 2026 · 4 min read

You already bought your next wave of jobs. You paid for them twice. Once when you spent on ads, trucks, and a sign to make a stranger call you. Again when you earned a customer's trust and put him in your files. The work of getting found is the expensive part, and you already did it. The money that leaks out now is in what happens after the phone rings and after the job is done.

Two habits decide whether that paid-for growth turns into revenue: how fast you respond, and how consistently you follow up. For a business your size, letting those two slip quietly costs $50,000 to $150,000 a year. Here is where it goes.

The leads you already bought are getting cold

When a lead comes in, the clock starts, and it is brutal. Research from Harvard Business Review found that a company responding within 5 minutes is 21 times more likely to qualify that lead than one that waits 30 minutes. Industry data shows about 78% of customers buy from the first business that responds.

So every ad dollar you spend buys a lead with a short fuse. Answer in five minutes and it is gold. Let it sit in a voicemail until you are off the truck, and you paid full price for a lead and handed the sale to whoever picked up first. You paid for that lead. The sale is decided in the minutes between the ring and your callback.

The list you already built is the lowest-cost growth you have

Now look at the names already in your system. Industry research puts the odds of selling to an existing customer at 60% to 70%, against 5% to 20% for a brand-new prospect. Winning a new customer runs 5 to 7 times what it costs to bring an old one back.

A past customer already trusts you, already knows your trucks, already paid you once. A short, steady rhythm of staying in front of that list, a seasonal reminder, a check-in, a note when something is due, turns names you already paid for into booked work at almost no cost. Most shops build that list for years and then let it sit silent.

The leak is speed and consistency

Both levers fail for the same two quiet reasons.

Speed fails because nobody owns the first response. The call comes in while everyone is busy, and "I'll get back to them" becomes tomorrow. Consistency fails because remarketing lives on a good intention instead of a calendar. There is always a fire to put out, so the follow-up that was not urgent simply never happens.

Neither one shows up on your profit and loss. There is no line item called "leads we answered too late" or "customers we never called again." That is exactly why they run for years without anyone catching them.

What to do this week

Pick the two lowest-cost moves first.

  • Put one person, or one system, in charge of answering every new lead inside five minutes during business hours. Measure nothing else yet, just the speed of that first response.
  • Pull your customer list and send one simple, useful touch to everyone you have not spoken to in a year. No discount, just a reason to think of you.

That is the whole week-one list. This is collecting on the leads already sitting in your phone and the customers already in your files.

Common questions

I already get plenty of leads. Why start here instead of buying more?

Because more leads pour into the same leak. If a slow callback is losing the leads you have, a bigger ad budget just buys you more leads to lose. Fix the speed and the follow-up first, and every dollar you have already spent starts working harder.

How fast is fast enough on a new lead?

Minutes, not hours. The research is blunt: inside 5 minutes you are 21 times more likely to qualify the lead than at 30 minutes, and most buyers go with whoever answers first. If you cannot reliably hit five minutes yourself, that is the clearest sign a system should catch the call for you.

What does "consistent" remarketing actually mean?

A set rhythm your office owns, run whether or not work is slow. A quarterly touch to your whole list beats a frantic email blast the month revenue dips. Steady and boring wins here, because your name stays in front of people who already chose you once.
