The Hidden Cost of the Owner Doing $15 Work

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · July 23, 2026 · 4 min read

The most overpriced labor in your business is you doing $15 work. Every hour the owner spends chasing invoices, booking appointments, and answering routine calls is an hour not spent selling jobs, running the crew, or closing the big quote. Industry averages and simple math put that cost at $2,000 to $7,000 a month for most owner-operators. The task got done. It just got done by the most expensive person in the building.

Your hour has a price, even when no one bills it

When you file paperwork for an hour, it feels free because no invoice changes hands. It is not free. That hour had a price: the job you would have sold, the crew problem you would have caught, the quote you would have followed up on. Economists call it opportunity cost. On a truck it just looks like a busy owner falling behind on the work only he can do. The task is cheap. Your time is not.

The trap of I can just do it faster

Owners hold onto low-value work because handing it off feels slower than doing it. For one task, that is true. Across a month, it is how you end up personally answering the phone instead of building the business that runs without you. The question is not whether you can do the $15 task. It is what you stopped doing to fit it in.

What the swap is worth

Put a real number on your hour, based on the work only you can do. Now count the hours a week you spend on tasks a $15 assistant or a simple system could handle. Multiply. For most owners the answer is thousands a month in higher-value work displaced by low-value busywork. That is not a scheduling problem. It is the single most overpaid labor line you have, and it does not show up anywhere on the books.

Where to start

You do not have to hand off everything at once. Start with the tasks that repeat and do not need judgment: booking, reminders, first-touch call answering, routine follow-up. Move them to a person or a system built for them. Keep the work that actually needs you. The goal is not a bigger team. It is your hours back on the jobs that only you can move.

Common questions

I am the cheapest labor I have. Why not do it myself?

You are the most expensive labor you have, once you count the work you give up to do the small task. Your hour is worth what you could earn selling and running jobs in it, not the wage of the task itself.

What should I hand off first?

The repeating tasks that do not need your judgment: appointment booking, reminders, answering routine calls, and standard follow-up. They are the easiest to move to a system or an assistant and the ones eating the most of your day.

Does this mean hiring, which I cannot afford?

Not necessarily. A lot of the low-value work can move to a simple automated setup before you ever add payroll. The point is getting it off your plate, whether that is a person or a system.

The task cost fifteen dollars. Doing it yourself cost the two hundred you were not out there earning.
