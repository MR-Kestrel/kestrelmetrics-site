The November Cliff

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Work was strong through October. Then one Wednesday in November you looked at the week ahead and there was nothing on it. No crew assignment, no deposit, no job on the board. The quiet stretch from November into February can drain a home-service shop of $30,000 to $90,000 in work that simply never gets booked. That is a built example, not a number pulled from your books, and the real figure rides on your ticket and how lean your winter runs. A slow season does not create that leak. It only makes a leak you already had impossible to ignore.

Where the quiet actually comes from

Most shops that hit an empty week in November spent the whole year running on one channel. Word of mouth. One lead app. One builder who kept the crew booked. It carried you in June because the entire market was moving at once. When the season turns, that single channel cools off for everyone on it at the same time, and there is nothing behind it to catch the drop.

We hear it straight from owners in our research. One told us close to 80% of his work came through a single app, and that app had started charging just to post. When your jobs all ride on one source, that source decides whether you work at all.

The math of an empty week

Here is the arithmetic in your own numbers. Say your average job runs $600 and a good week books eight of them. That is about $4,800 in a single week of work. Lose half of your normal booking across eight lean winter weeks and you have roughly $19,000 in work that walked out the door. Add the jobs that went to the shop that answered when you had nothing to say, run it lean two winters running, and a built range of $30,000 to $90,000 a year is not hard to reach.

Change the ticket and the number moves. The shape does not. A quiet channel costs you every week it stays quiet, and the bill comes due in the spring, when the busy season has to pay for the one before it.

Build the second channel while the first one still pays

The shops that coast through winter did the work back in the summer. This is a busy-season rule, not a slow-season scramble. When the phone is ringing is exactly when you have the cash, the crew, and the calm to stand up a second way for work to find you. A channel you start in November is a channel that pays you in March.

The trap is waiting until the week is already empty. Now you are trying to build the pipe and fill it at the same time, with less money in the bank and more fear in the room. Pick one more source while channel one is still paying the bills: a referral ask with a real reason behind it, or a second platform you actually work instead of just list on.

Work the list before the phone goes quiet

You are sitting on the cheapest work you will ever book, and most shops never touch it. The customers you already served know you, trust you, and have paid you once. A short, plain reach-out to last year's list, sent before the season turns, will surface more booked jobs than any cold lead, at a fraction of the cost to win them.

The owners who stay busy in the quiet months did not find a magic source. They worked the list they already had while everyone else sat waiting for the phone.

Common questions

Is this just a slow season everyone has to ride out?

Some seasonal dip is real. The part you control is how far you fall. A shop with two or three working sources of work feels a slow November as a lighter month. A shop with one source feels it as an empty week. The season sets the tide. Your channel count decides whether you float or scrape bottom.

I don't have time to build anything new right now. Where do I start?

Start with the list you already have. One message to past customers costs you an hour and no ad money, and it pulls from people who already trusted you with a job. It is the fastest work to book and the first move to make before the phone goes quiet.

How do I know which channel to add?

Look at where your best jobs came from this year, then find the nearest second version of it. If word of mouth carries you, a referral system makes it repeatable. If one app feeds you, a past-customer campaign gives you work that app can never charge you for. Add the one you can actually keep up with.
