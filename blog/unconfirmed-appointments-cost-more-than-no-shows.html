Unconfirmed Appointments Cost More Than No-Shows

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · July 23, 2026 · 4 min read

An unconfirmed appointment is a coin flip you keep paying to lose. Industry averages put no-shows near a quarter of all service bookings, and unconfirmed ones fare worse. A single confirmation the day before cuts that rate by a third to a half. For a busy shop, the gap between confirmed and unconfirmed is worth $1,500 to $6,000 a month in slots that either got filled or got wasted.

The empty slot is not free

When a customer no-shows, you do not lose nothing. You lose the paid tech who drove out and stood still, the fuel to get there, and the other customer you turned away because that time was taken. One empty slot costs you twice: once for the job that did not happen, and once for the job you could have booked in its place. That is the real price, and it hides because the calendar still looked full yesterday.

Unconfirmed is the same as unsure

An appointment nobody confirmed is a plan the customer made and may have forgotten. Life moved, something came up, and no one closed the loop. Industry figures suggest confirmed appointments show up far more reliably than unconfirmed ones. The booking on your calendar is only worth as much as the confirmation behind it. Without one, you are dispatching a truck on a maybe.

What a confirmation is worth

The fix is close to free. A reminder the day before, by text or call, and a quick yes from the customer. Industry data suggests that single step cuts no-shows by roughly a third to a half. Put your average ticket and your tech's hourly cost on the slots you save, and one confirmation habit pays for itself many times over. This is not a scheduling nicety. It is money left on the calendar.

Why it slips

Confirmations slip for the usual reason. They depend on a busy person remembering to make one more call at the end of a full day. So they do not happen, and the calendar quietly fills with coin flips. Take it off the person. An automatic reminder the day before, with an easy way to confirm or reschedule, turns maybes into kept jobs without anyone having to remember.

Common questions

What is a normal no-show rate?

Across service industries the average sits near a quarter of bookings. Trades with unconfirmed appointments often run higher. If you are not tracking it, that is the first number to start counting.

When should the confirmation go out?

The day before works best for most trades, close enough that the customer remembers and far enough that they can reschedule instead of vanishing. A second reminder the morning of helps for high-value jobs.

Does a reschedule count as a loss?

No. A reschedule keeps the job and frees the slot for someone else. That is the point of confirming: you turn a silent no-show into a kept appointment or an honest reschedule, and either one beats an empty truck.

A booking you never confirmed is not a job. It is a coin flip you paid to make.
