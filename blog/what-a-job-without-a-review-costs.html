What a Job That Ends Without a Review Costs You

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · September 4, 2026 · 4 min read

You did the work. The customer paid and was glad you came. Then nothing. No review, no rating, no public word that you exist and you are good. Most home services businesses capture a review on fewer than 1 in 10 completed jobs. Each silent job forfeits a small, permanent asset that would have paid for itself many times over in calls you never had to buy.

The leak nobody logs

A missed review does not show up anywhere. There is no line item, no report, no angry customer. The job closed, the money came in, and the leak is simply the thing that did not happen. That is what makes it the easiest leak in the business to ignore and one of the most expensive to leave running. You cannot count what you never asked for, so it never enters the conversation.

Why a review is worth more than the star

A review does two jobs at once. It moves you up in the local map results, where most home services calls now start, and it convinces the next stranger to pick you over the shop with fewer or older reviews. A business with a steady flow of recent reviews shows up more often and gets chosen more often. Both effects compound. This month's reviews are still working for you a year from now, which is the opposite of an ad that stops the day you stop paying.

The math on the ones you skip

Say you close 1,500 jobs a year and ask for a review on almost none of them. Turn that into a simple habit that captures even 25 percent, and you add hundreds of new reviews a year. For a typical home services business, a steady review engine is worth roughly $1,000 to $4,000 a month in additional booked work, at close to zero cost per lead. The exact figure is yours to measure. The point is that it is not zero, and right now it is running out the door on every job that ends in silence.

How to stop the leak this week

You do not need software to start. Decide who asks, when they ask, and exactly what they say. The best moment is the end of the visit, in person, while the customer is standing in the fixed result. A single text with a direct link, sent the same day, does most of the rest. Ask on every completed job, track the rate for 30 days, and the leak turns into a number you can grow.

Common questions

How many of my completed jobs should turn into reviews?

Most businesses capture a review on fewer than 10 percent of jobs without a system. A simple, consistent ask at the end of every visit moves that to 25 to 40 percent, which is enough to change how often you show up and get chosen.

What is a review actually worth?

A single review is a permanent asset that keeps working. For a typical home services business, a steady flow of new reviews is worth roughly $1,000 to $4,000 a month in additional booked work, because it lifts both your ranking and your close rate at close to zero cost per lead.

Do I need special tools to fix this?

No. You need a decision about who asks, when, and what they say, plus a same-day text with a direct link. Tools help you scale later. The leak closes the day you start asking on every job.

A finished job with no review is a sale you completed and an asset you threw away.
