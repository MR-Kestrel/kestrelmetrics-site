What Answering Every Call Is Actually Worth

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · July 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Answering every call is worth more than almost anything else you could do this month. Industry studies suggest a large share of home services calls go unanswered, and most of those callers never dial back. Close that gap and the recovered work runs $3,000 to $12,000 a month for a typical shop. This is not about being busier. It is about keeping the jobs that already tried to reach you and got voicemail instead.

Every ring is a customer with money out

People do not call a plumber to chat. They call because water is somewhere it should not be, and they have a credit card ready. A ringing phone is a customer with money out and patience running short. Miss it and that money does not wait. Industry figures suggest most callers who reach voicemail simply call the next number, and a large share of home services calls come in after hours, exactly when nobody is there to answer. The demand showed up. The shop did not.

The math on the calls you keep

Take the calls you miss in a month. Even a conservative share of them would have booked a job. Put your average ticket on that share and the number gets loud. A shop missing a dozen calls a month at a few hundred dollars a job is leaving a new-hire's wages on the table, every month, in work that was already trying to become revenue. The calls you answer are the ones that fill the calendar. The calls you keep are the difference between a good month and a flat one.

Captured, not chased

There is a difference between chasing new demand and capturing the demand you already have. Ads chase. Answering captures. The captured call costs you nothing extra, because you already paid to make the phone ring, through your name, your reviews, your years in the trade. Letting it go to voicemail is paying to generate a lead and then handing it to a competitor at the last step. Capture is the cheapest revenue in the building.

What answering every call actually takes

It does not take you personally catching every ring. It takes making sure something answers, every time, including the nights and weekends when the emergencies land. A live answering setup, an after-hours booking, an automated text-back that catches the caller before they dial the next shop. The goal is simple and measurable: no call goes unanswered, and no answered call goes unbooked.

Common questions

What share of calls do businesses usually miss?

Industry studies of tracked home services lines suggest the missed share is large, often a majority in a given month once after-hours calls are counted. Most owners guess low, because the phone still feels busy. The only way to know yours is to measure it.

Do I have to answer calls myself to fix this?

No. You have to make sure a call is always answered, which is different. A live service or an automated text-back that books the appointment beats voicemail by a wide margin and does not chain you to the phone.

Are after-hours calls really worth answering?

Yes. A large share of home services demand shows up evenings and weekends, and those callers have an emergency and no patience. Catch them and you win the job. Miss them and most will not try you twice.

The phone already rang. Whether that call became revenue or a competitor's job came down to who answered.
