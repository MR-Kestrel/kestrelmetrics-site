Your Dormant Customer List Is Cash You Already Earned

By Michael Reaves · Kestrel Metrics · July 23, 2026 · 4 min read

The cheapest jobs you will book this year are already in your phone. Industry averages suggest a home services business can rebook $2,000 to $9,000 a month in work from past customers who have simply gone quiet. Winning back someone who already paid you costs a fraction of what you spend to land a stranger. The list is not old contacts. It is earned money you have not gone back to collect.

Cold does not mean gone

A customer who has not called in a year is not unhappy. He forgot, or life got busy, or the reminder to service the unit never came. Most dormant customers left on good terms and would use you again if you gave them a reason. They are sitting in plain sight in your records, worth real work, waiting on a call you have not made.

Why they are cheaper than new leads

A stranger costs money to find, has no reason to trust you, and compares you against three other shops. A past customer already knows your name, has your number, and remembers the job you did. Industry averages suggest keeping and reactivating existing customers costs far less than acquiring new ones. You are not starting a sale from zero. You are picking one back up where it went quiet.

The math on a list you already own

Say you have 300 past customers and you have not touched most of them in a year. Reach out and even a small share will book something, a tune-up, a repair they have been putting off, the next season's service. Put your average ticket on that slice and the number is larger than a month of ad spend, for the cost of a few texts and calls. This is banked work, and it is the fastest dollar in the building.

Why the list goes cold

It goes cold for the same reason quotes vanish. Nobody owns the follow-up, so it lives in memory, and memory is busy running jobs. The names pile up and go quiet, not because anyone decided to let them, but because nothing reached back out. Fix it with a standing rhythm: a seasonal reminder, a check-in, a reason to call. The list only pays when something works it.

Common questions

What do I even say to a customer I have not talked to in a year?

Keep it useful and short. A seasonal service reminder, a check on the work you did, an offer tied to the time of year. You are not apologizing for the gap. You are giving them a reason to book now.

How often should I reach out to past customers?

A few times a year is plenty for most trades, tied to seasons and service intervals. The point is a steady rhythm so the list never goes fully cold again, not a flood of messages.

Is a discount required to bring them back?

No. Often a simple reminder at the right time is enough, because they meant to call and forgot. Save the offer for the ones who need an extra push, and lead with the reminder.

You already earned these customers once. They are still yours, until a competitor calls them first.
